INTERPRETATION NOTES
--------------------------------------------------------------------------------
The year fixed effects models compare countries to each other while controlling for global year shocks. The country and year fixed effects models are stronger because they compare each country more closely against itself over time. This helps control for time-invariant country characteristics such as geography, long-run institutional differences, and persistent regional security conditions.

The coefficient on lagged military spending should be interpreted as an association, not as a causal effect. A positive coefficient may reflect threat-driven spending, where countries experiencing more terrorism risk increase military and security expenditure in response.

Because terrorist attacks and deaths are count-based outcomes with many small values and a few very large values, later versions of this project may also use Poisson or negative binomial models as robustness checks.
